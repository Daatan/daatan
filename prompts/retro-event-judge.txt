You read news reports that a clustering step grouped together. Decide whether they describe ONE discrete event that has already happened. If they do, state the event and write the forecast question a careful forecaster could have asked a week before it happened, without knowing the outcome.

Each report is one sentence a reader extracted from an article, with the article's publication date and outlet, and sometimes a quote from the article. Some sentences end with a clause that relates the event to an unrelated forecast ("a precursor to …", "which cuts against …"); ignore that clause.

Step 1. kind: pick exactly one.
- "event": most reports say that one specific thing happened at a specific time: a decision, vote, appointment, resignation, strike, attack, signed deal, court ruling, launch, official announcement or published figure.
- "routine": one specific thing happened, as for "event", but it is one more of a kind that recurs as a matter of course, or something nobody could have seen coming a week before: another prisoner swap, another missile, drone or shelling attack on a city or its infrastructure (even a heavy one, or on a new target), another call or meeting in a running series, another launch or test; an earthquake, a crash, a fire. Pick "event" for such a thing only when something about this one was distinct and known in advance: the talks, summit, vote or deadline it was tied to, or that it set a record ("the largest since …").
- "trend": a gradual change or a running state over a period, with no single moment it happened: prices falling for days, fund flows, polls drifting, a war or a negotiation going on.
- "not_yet": the reports say it is scheduled, planned, expected or threatened, but not that it has happened.
- "mixed": the reports describe different events that only share a topic or actors.
- "other": opinion, analysis, what someone thinks or wants, or anything else.
For any kind other than "event", leave every text field below empty and set eventIndex to 0.

Step 2. The event (kind "event" only).
- eventSummary: one sentence, past tense, plain English: who did what. Use only what the reports say.
- eventDate: the day it happened, YYYY-MM-DD, from the reports' own wording and dates ("on Tuesday" in an article published Thursday 2026-11-12 means 2026-11-10). When no report names the day, use the earliest publication date of a report that says it has happened. Empty string only when you cannot tell even that.
- eventIndex: the number of the report that most directly confirms that it happened.

Step 3. The forecast question (kind "event" only), as someone would have asked it a week before the event.
- claim: one declarative sentence in the future tense that the event makes true, ending with "by {DEADLINE}". Write the literal token {DEADLINE}; the date is filled in later. Example: "Israel's President will task Gadi Eisenkot with forming the next government by {DEADLINE}." When the event was a refusal, a rejection or something withheld, the claim says so ("United Torah Judaism will decline to recommend a candidate …"); never ask about its opposite.
- Ask at the level of detail that people argued about before the event, no finer and no coarser. A finer question is a lottery nobody could call; a coarser one would have come true anyway.
- Time: never the exact day. When the day was set in advance (an election, a scheduled vote, a summit, a deadline), ask about that occasion: "at the talks in Abu Dhabi", "in the Knesset vote on the Speaker".
- Figures: never a count, amount or measure that the outcome revealed: how many votes, signatures, seats, prisoners, casualties or targets ("two bridges"), a magnitude, a price. Keep a threshold only when the line matters by itself, such as a majority, a quorum or a record, or when the reports say that number was expected or announced beforehand. Never pick a threshold that sits just under the outcome.
- Place and actors: name them as plainly as the reports do, at the level the matter was argued about: the country, city or institution rather than the exact spot, and a person only as one of the known sides or contenders.
- Form: ask about the decisive act, not its packaging ("an interim final rule", "with missiles", "a formal request"), unless the form itself was in dispute.
- A repeat with a distinct occasion: tie the question to that occasion or say "a new" or "another", so that an earlier one does not count.
- Ask about the event itself and nothing weaker: if the event was an official decision, ask about the decision, not about talks or reports of it. Give no hint that it happened.
- resolutionRules: two or three sentences. Say what counts as YES (the event as it actually happened, by {DEADLINE}, per official sources or credible news reports), and that the forecast resolves NO otherwise.

reason: one short sentence on why you chose this kind.
