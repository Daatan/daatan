You read this week's news reports, grouped by a clustering step: each group is one development that several outlets reported, and several groups often belong to one story. Find the stories people are following and write the forecast questions about what happens next that are still open today.

Each group has a number, the number of outlets that reported it, and a few of its reports: one sentence a reader extracted from an article, with the article's publication date and outlet, newest first. Some sentences end with a clause that relates the development to a forecast ("a precursor to …", "which cuts against …"); ignore that clause.

Step 1. Stories. Put groups about the same story into one story: the same actors and the same matter, such as a government formation, an election, a market selloff, a war, a company crisis or an investigation. A group belongs to at most one story. Leave out groups that are only opinion, analysis or old background.

Step 2. Questions. For each story, write up to three new questions about what happens next. Daatan usually already asks a story's headline question, such as whether a government will be formed, a war will end or a market will crash; those are in the open forecasts below. So do not stop at the headline question. Look in the reports for the concrete steps the story is waiting on: a mandate extension asked for or granted, a coalition agreement signed, a minister named, a vote held, charges filed, a ruling issued, a deal or treaty signed, a meeting held, an official figure published. Each such step that is still open, could go either way and is not settled by an open forecast is a question. Prefer fewer, better questions: most stories have one or two such steps, and many have none. A market or sports story rarely has one, because its next steps are prices and statistics, not decisions. A story with nothing open worth asking gets an empty list. Each question must be:
- Open today. Nothing in the reports settles it, either way. Never ask about something the reports say has already happened.
- Decided by one outcome that official sources or credible news will report: a decision, vote, appointment, signed agreement, charge, ruling, resignation, or an official figure crossing a line. Never ask whether something will stop, continue, stay or happen again (another strike, another attack, a new record, a run of losing days): such a claim comes true by default, cannot be confirmed, or is near-certain. Never ask about anyone's death or health.
- Worth following. Its answer changes how the story goes. Not a statistic along the way, such as a player's points, a rating gain or a price swing over a few days.
- Uncertain. The reports or the open forecasts show it could go either way. Skip outcomes that are near-certain, and ones nobody could call.
- New. The open forecasts below are the questions Daatan already asks about these stories. When one of them asks the same question, or one whose answer settles yours either way, set coveredBy to that forecast's number and leave claim, deadline and resolutionRules empty. A question that differs only in its deadline or a slightly different threshold is covered. A step on the way to a forecast's outcome is new unless that forecast's answer settles it: "Gadi Eisenkot will ask for an extension of his mandate" is new next to "a new government will be formed by June 30", because the answer to the second does not decide the first. In a market story, a question about another index, price or time window that moves with an open forecast is covered too. Covered questions do not count toward the three: after one, keep looking for a step that is new.

Fields of a question:
- claim: one declarative sentence in the future tense that ends with "by {DEADLINE}". Write the literal token {DEADLINE}; the date is filled in from deadline. Example: "Gadi Eisenkot will ask the President for an extension of his mandate to form a government by {DEADLINE}." Name the actors plainly. Never write a claim that comes true merely because nothing happened ("will not sign", "will fail to form"), or one about something stopping, lasting or repeating ("will cease attacks on Bahrain", "will remain Knesset speaker", "will conduct another strike", "will close 5% lower in one session").
- deadline: YYYY-MM-DD, the day by which the outcome will be known. When the matter sets its own date (a legal deadline, a scheduled vote, meeting or report), use that date; work it out when the reports give a start and a length, such as a 28-day mandate given on a known day. Otherwise use {{stepDeadline}} for a step on the way, and {{defaultDeadline}} only for a question about how the whole story ends. Never earlier than {{minDeadline}}.
- resolutionRules: two or three sentences. Say what counts as YES by {DEADLINE}, per official sources or credible news reports, and that the forecast resolves NO otherwise.
- coveredBy: 0, or the number of the open forecast that already asks this question.
- reason: one short sentence on why this question is open and worth asking now.

title: a short name for the story, a few words.

Answer with one JSON object and nothing else, in this shape: {"stories": [{"title": "…", "groups": [1, 4], "questions": [{"claim": "… by {DEADLINE}.", "deadline": "YYYY-MM-DD", "resolutionRules": "…", "coveredBy": 0, "reason": "…"}]}]}. "groups" lists the numbers of the groups in the story. Write in English.
